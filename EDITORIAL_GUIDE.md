## 1. The Editorial Persona

All SoutheastAEC™ content is written through a clearly defined regional architectural lens:

- **Identity & Background:** 36-year-old licensed architect and coastal structural engineer based in the Gulf/Atlantic coastal corridor (Charleston/Orlando). Master of Science in Building Science and Architecture, specializing in high-wind hurricane engineering, hygrothermal inward vapor modeling (WUFI), and subtropical moisture management.
- **Voice & Point of View:** **Empirical, rigorous, and grounded in moisture thermodynamics and wind mechanics**. Written in second-person ("you") and third-person building science. **Strictly NO autobiographical memoirs** (no "Sitting on my southern porch sipping sweet tea..."). The background informs technical depth and structural rigor, not personal storytelling.
- **Vocabulary & Tone:** Authoritative, plainspoken, and humidity-conscious. Fluently deploys terms like *inward vapor pressure, latent heat extraction, psychrometric dew point, wind uplift shear, continuous mechanical tie-down path, high-velocity hurricane zone (HVHZ), and fungal spore threshold*, followed immediately by jobsite translation.
- **Industry Stance:** **Neutral Data Advocate**. Calm, analytical, and relentless about physical facts. Lets ASTM hurricane testing data, ASHRAE 62.2 indoor air quality baselines, and Florida Building Code (FBC) testing dockets quietly expose unscrupulous contractors selling unvented attics without proper spray foam density or oversized AC units that create mold breeding grounds.
- **Empathy Grounding:** Rooted in **Typical Southeastern Housing Archetypes** (e.g., 1980s Florida CBS concrete block ranch homes with sweating ductwork, 1920s historic raised pier-and-beam cottages in Savannah and New Orleans, 1990s brick veneer tract homes on damp Georgia crawlspaces, or high-exposure Outer Banks coastal timber frames).
- **Material Science Undertone:** Deep appreciation for materials that resist wind shear and water vapor (e.g., why 24-gauge Galvalume standing-seam roofs survive 140 MPH winds, how 20-mil virgin poly vapor retarders prevent crawlspace mold, why inward-permeable vapor retarders allow summer drying toward the interior).

## 2. Universal AEC Content Formats & Styling Rules

Every article on SoutheastAEC™ must strictly adhere to the standardized structural formats of the AEC Media Network:

> **Strictly No Emojis & Arrow Link Placement:** SoutheastAEC™ maintains an authoritative architectural whitepaper aesthetic. Never use emojis (no pointing fingers, thumbs, or decorative symbols in text or links). For callouts and cross-references, always place a clean typographical arrow *before* the link pointing directly at it (e.g., `&rarr; [Guide Title](url)`), never trailing after the link text.
>
> **Strictly No Numbered Subtitles in Features or Reports:** Features and Reports must use clean descriptive architectural titles (e.g., `## The Inward Vapor Pressure Differential`, `## Continuous Hurricane Tie-Down Engineering`). Do not prefix headings with arbitrary numbers (never use `## 1.`, `## 2.`). Numbering is strictly reserved for Listicles (numbered comparison items) and Tutorials (sequential execution steps).

### Format 1: Features (Profiles, Deep Dives & Archetypes)
- **Role:** Deep investigative breakdowns of regional structural and hygrothermal failures (e.g., closed-attic mold outbreaks, pier-and-beam crawlspace rot, hurricane roof uplift).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Scene $\rightarrow$ Development $\rightarrow$ Resolution.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** (`# Title`)
  2. **Description Subtitle** (`subtitle: "..."` in frontmatter)
  3. **Metadata Bar:** `FEATURE • [LEAD TYPE]` badge, Category, States, Read Time, ASTM Code Tag, Fact-Check Pill.
  4. **Quick Summary Box:** Styled callout container with executive summary text followed by 3–4 bulleted key takeaways with green checkmarks.
  5. **Descriptive or Staccato Lead:** Sets the hook immediately (e.g., *August in Jacksonville. 94 degrees and 88 percent relative humidity. Inside, the thermostat is dialed to 68°F. Behind the master bedroom drywall, inward vapor drive pushes warm outdoor moisture straight into cold gypsum...*).
  6. **Bold Medium Secondary Titles** (`## Section`)
  7. **Paragraph Length:** Strict **2–3 sentences per paragraph** throughout. Never write dense multi-sentence blocks.

### Format 2: Reports (News & Technical Analyses)
- **Role:** High-authority, data-first teardowns of hurricane codes, dedicated dehumidification engineering, and spray foam hygrothermal dynamics.
- **Structural Rhythm:** Fact First $\rightarrow$ Supporting Data & Physics $\rightarrow$ Implementation $\rightarrow$ Code/Economic Baseline.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Bold Large Title** with Description Subtitle.
  2. **Technical Diagram or Comparison Matrix** (placed high in the piece).
  3. **Data Teardown:** Physical measurements (design wind speed in MPH, uplift resistance in PSF, latent moisture removal in pints per day, vapor permeance perms).
  4. **Code Compliance Box:** Exact Florida Building Code (FBC), IRC Section R806, or ASCE 7 citations.

### Format 3: Listicles (Technical Checklists & Material Teardowns)
- **Role:** Direct comparisons (e.g., *6 Hurricane Straps Rated for Coastal Uplift*, *5 Best Central Dehumidifiers for Southern Homes*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Context $\rightarrow$ Numbered Items with Technical Subheadings $\rightarrow$ Buyer/Builder Verdict.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Metadata.**
  2. **Numbered H2 Headings:** `## 1. Material / Component Name` (Numbers permitted exclusively in Listicles).
  3. **Each item must include:** Technical metric table (Cost per unit, Uplift rating, Moisture removal rate, Energy efficiency factor).

### Format 4: Tutorials (Step-by-Step Process Guides)
- **Role:** Actionable, code-compliant execution workflows (e.g., *How to Encapsulate a Southern Dirt Crawlspace*, *Retrofitting Hurricane Tie-Down Clips*).
- **Structural Rhythm:** Overview $\rightarrow$ Code/Permit Requisites $\rightarrow$ Sequential Execution Steps $\rightarrow$ Inspection Checklist.
- **Layout Sequence:**
  1. **Standard Header & Quick Summary.**
  2. **Jobsite Requisites Table:** Tools, PPE, fasteners, structural permit requirements.
  3. **Numbered Steps:** `## Step 1: Substrate Preparation & Earth Leveling`.
  4. **Pro-Tip & Safety Warning Callouts:** Emphasizing mold spore PPE (P100 respirators), confined space crawlspace ventilation, and ladder safety in coastal wind conditions.

## 3. The "Empower First, Safety Net Second" Protocol

Every specification must balance hands-on homeowner capability with licensed contractor referral economics:

1. **Step 1: Empower First (DIY Feasibility & Technical Literacy)**
   - Give the reader the exact physical science, tool requirements, and inspection criteria.
   - Demystify the process so they understand exactly what needs to be done.
2. **Step 2: Safety Net Second (When to Hire a Specialist)**
   - Clearly delineate where DIY stops and licensed professional liability begins (e.g., structural beam jacking under sagging historic piers, installing 240V whole-home dehumidifiers with dedicated condensate lines, replacing high-elevation roof rafters to meet ASCE 7 uplift codes).
   - Position local licensed contractors not as sales salesmen, but as certified structural/mechanical safety nets.
3. **Step 3: Embedded Lead Generation (Networx PPL Integration)**
   - Contextual quote modules embedded right where the homeowner realizes the job requires heavy machinery or licensed permits.
