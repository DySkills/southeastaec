How to Level a Pier-and-Beam Foundation: Hydraulic Bottle Jacks, Cribbing, and Steel Shims

A step-by-step contractor protocol for setting heavy timber cribbing, operating 20-ton bottle jacks, and re-shimming settling piers in 1/8-inch micro-lifts.

By SoutheastAEC™ Building Science • Published October 2026 • OSHA 1926 Subpart T / ASCE 11-99 / IRC Section R403
Quick Summary

Re-leveling a historic Southern home on an elevated pier-and-beam foundation requires precise hydraulic engineering and an uncompromising commitment to structural safety. Jacking a house too quickly, using undersized jacks, or resting hydraulic cylinders directly on native dirt will cause sudden cribbing collapse, catastrophic plaster cracking, and severe structural frame racking. Following this field-tested, five-phase procedure distributes dead loads safely across solid oak timber cribbing towers, executes synchronized micro-lifts limited to 1/8 inch per 24 hours, and establishes permanent bearing points using galvanized steel shims and non-shrink structural grout.

  • Temporary Cribbing Foundation Mandate: Hydraulic jacks must never rest directly on bare ground; jacks must bear upon wide timber cribbing pads that distribute concentrated point loads across the soil.
  • The 1/8-Inch Daily Micro-Lift Rule: Restricting vertical elevation changes to a maximum of 1/8 inch every 24 hours allows antique lath, lime plaster, and mortise-and-tenon joints to adjust without fractures.
  • Hydraulic Load Rating Standard: Only heavy-duty 20-ton or 30-ton commercial bottle jacks equipped with positive locking nuts or auxiliary safety stands should be deployed beneath active framing.
  • Permanent Bearing Hardware: Wood shingles and soft pine scrap wedges must never be used for permanent shimming; only 1/4-inch to 1/2-inch hot-dip galvanized steel plates provide non-crushable permanent support.

Jobsite Requisites, Hydraulic Equipment, and Shoring Materials

House lifting involves massive live and dead loads concentrated within confined crawlspace quarters. You must assemble commercial-grade structural lifting machinery and rigid safety cribbing prior to touching any hydraulic valves.

Critical Confined Space Crushing Hazard

Never position your body directly beneath an unsupported wooden girder while a hydraulic jack holds the load. A hydraulic seal can blow without warning, causing the beam to drop instantly. You must follow the jack stroke continuously with solid oak safety blocking slipped between the timber and the cribbing tower.

Step-by-Step Foundation Re-Leveling Protocol

Step 1: Baseline Elevation Mapping and Plaster Audit

Establish a permanent benchmark elevation at the home's highest structural point (frequently the front masonry steps or a stable perimeter corner pier). Using an electronic digital level or clear vinyl water level, map the elevation offset at every exterior wall corner, interior door threshold, and pier location throughout the home.

Record all elevation readings on a detailed grid map of the framing footprint. Calculate the total differential settlement between the highest point and the lowest sagging pier bay.

Walk through every room and photograph existing wall cracks, window frames, and plaster ceilings. Documenting pre-existing cracks ensures you can differentiate historic settling fissures from new hairline stress fractures during the jacking process.

Step 2: Setting Timber Cribbing and Ground Distribution Pads

Excavate and tamp flat a 3-foot by 3-foot area of crawlspace earth immediately adjacent to the settling pier. Never set hydraulic jacks directly on native soil, as the 20-ton point load will simply drive the jack deep into the mud rather than lifting the house.

Lay a continuous base pad of two 2x12 pressure-treated ground-contact planks or 4x8-inch dense oak timbers directly across the leveled dirt. This wide timber footing distributes concentrated jacking loads across a broad surface area, keeping soil bearing pressures below 1,000 PSF.

Erect a heavy-duty box-crib tower directly on top of the base pad using 6x6 solid oak timbers stacked in alternating cross-hatched pairs. Continue stacking timber tiers until the top of the cribbing tower sits approximately 10 to 14 inches below the bottom of the sagging girder.

Step 3: Positioning the 20-Ton Bottle Jack and Header Plate

Center a commercial-grade 20-ton hydraulic bottle jack on top of the oak cribbing tower directly beneath the sagging timber girder. Ensure the jack's base sits perfectly flat and plumb to prevent lateral kick-out under heavy compression.

Place an 8x8-inch by 3/4-inch thick Grade 50 structural steel distribution plate across the top saddle of the jack piston. The steel plate prevents the concentrated 2-inch diameter steel jack piston from crushing upward into the soft grain of the wooden beam.

Rotate the jack's manual screw extension collar upward until the steel distribution plate contacts the bottom face of the girder firmly. Verify with a small spirit level that the jack piston and cribbing tower remain perfectly perpendicular to the floor framing.

Step 4: Executing the Synchronized 1/8-Inch Daily Lift

Close the hydraulic release valve tightly using the notched jack operating handle. Pump the handle slowly while watching the hydraulic pressure gauge rise as the cylinder assumes the structural dead load of the building.

Observe the junction between the girder and the settled brick pier as the timber begins to lift free. Stop pumping immediately once the girder rises exactly one-eighth of an inch (1/8") above its settled resting position.

Immediately slip hardwood oak safety wedges or steel spacer plates into the resulting gap between the timber and the brick pier. Do not leave the house supported exclusively on active hydraulic fluid pressure; the load must be shared by solid mechanical blocking.

Allow the structure to rest at this new elevation for a full 24 hours. This mandatory waiting period allows the timber framing, diagonal subflooring, and brittle interior plaster lath to relieve internal stresses gradually without rupturing.

Step 5: Setting Permanent Galvanized Steel Shims and Grouting

Repeat the 1/8-inch daily lifting procedure over consecutive days until the digital elevation meter indicates the floor frame has returned to its target level elevation. Check interior doors and trim frequently to verify that pocket doors swing freely and door frames square up.

Once final target elevation is achieved, measure the exact vertical clearance remaining between the bottom of the girder and the top surface of the masonry pier. Select a combination of hot-dip galvanized structural steel shims (available in 1/8", 1/4", and 1/2" increments) to fill the gap.

Slide a continuous 26-gauge Type 304 stainless steel termite shield over the pier, followed by the galvanized steel shims, ensuring the shims support the full width of the timber beam. Mix high-strength non-shrink precision cementitious grout (such as Quikrete Non-Shrink Precision Grout or MasterFlow 585) to a packable consistency and pack it tightly around the shims beneath the bearing plate.

Allow the non-shrink structural grout to cure for at least 48 hours to reach its rated compressive strength of over 5,000 PSI. Once cured, slowly open the hydraulic jack release valve to lower the girder onto its permanent steel and grout foundation, then disassemble and remove the temporary cribbing tower.

Empower First: Post-Leveling Structural Monitoring

Homeowners can verify the ongoing stability of their re-leveled foundation through simple visual checkpoints:

  • Check Door Alignment and Latch Engagement: Test all interior doors in the lifted zone. A properly leveled frame allows doors to swing smoothly without self-closing or binding against the jamb strike plate.
  • Inspect Plaster Seams for Dynamic Cracking: Place a small piece of painter's tape across any preexisting plaster cracks. If the tape tears or wrinkles over the following weeks, the framing is still settling or shifting.
  • Perform Monthly Crawlspace Pier Audits: Crawl the foundation cavity 30 days after jacking to verify that the steel shims have not shifted and that the non-shrink grout bed shows zero crumbling or displacement.
  • Monitor Seasonal Rainwater Ponding: Confirm that exterior downspouts and yard grades direct water at least six feet away from foundation piers to prevent soil saturation around new footings.

Safety Net Second: When to Engage a Licensed Structural Contractor

Foundation jacking carries immense structural liability and life-safety hazards that demand licensed engineering oversight when specific failure thresholds are reached:

  • Multiple Interconnected Settling Bays: When three or more central girders have settled across an entire historic residence, jacking a single point will twist the building's floor diaphragm. A licensed house-moving or structural shoring contractor must deploy multi-point unified hydraulic jacking machines to lift the entire building concurrently.
  • Crumbled or Leaning Perimeter Brick Piers: If exterior brick piers are out of plumb by more than two inches or display loose, crumbling lime mortar, attempting to jack against them will cause lateral masonry blowouts; a licensed contractor must rebuild piers from reinforced concrete spread footings up.
  • Sub-Surface Sinkholes or Unconsolidated Muck: When soil tests reveal deep subterranean organic peat, muck, or voids extending below eight feet, surface spread footings will continue to sink; a deep-foundation contractor must drive hydraulically rotated steel helical piles deep into load-bearing sand or limestone strata.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use wood blocks to permanently shim my floor joists?

No, you must never use wood scraps, untreated pine 2x4s, or cedar shingles as permanent shims under load-bearing girders. The immense concentrated weight of the house will crush soft wood fibers over time, causing the floor to sag again within months, and untreated wood shims provide a direct food source for subterranean termites.

How much can you safely lift a house each day?

The industry-accepted building science limit for lifting an occupied home with existing plaster walls is one-eighth of an inch (1/8") per 24-hour period. Exceeding this rate does not allow historic wood framing, lath, and brittle lime plaster time to flex and normalize, resulting in severe interior wall cracking and binding door frames.

What size bottle jack do I need to level a pier-and-beam house?

A commercial-grade 20-ton or 30-ton hydraulic bottle jack is the minimum recommended size for residential leveling. While a 12-ton jack can technically lift localized framing, a 20-ton unit operates comfortably within its hydraulic safety envelope, provides smoother lift control, and resists sudden seal blowouts.

Why does non-shrink grout need to be used under shims?

Standard concrete mortar shrinks slightly as water evaporates during curing, which creates microscopic air voids between the shims and the masonry pier that allow the girder to settle. Non-shrink precision structural grout (ASTM C1107) expands microscopically as it cures, ensuring 100% solid, void-free contact capable of bearing over 5,000 to 8,000 PSI of structural load.
