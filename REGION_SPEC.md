## 1. Geographic Scope & Thermodynamic Climate Profile

- **Target States (11 States):** Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana, East Texas, Arkansas, Virginia.
- **IECC / ASHRAE 90.1 Climate Zones:**
  - **Zone 2A (Hot - Humid):** Central and South Florida, Louisiana Gulf Coast, coastal Texas, coastal Mississippi/Alabama. Extreme cooling degree days, annual rainfall exceeding 55 to 65 inches, and prolonged summer dew points above 72°F to 76°F.
  - **Zone 3A (Warm - Humid):** North Florida, Georgia, South Carolina, eastern North Carolina, southern Alabama/Mississippi. Long, punishingly humid summers and short, mild winters. Constant inward vapor drive during cooling months.
  - **Zone 4A (Mixed - Humid):** Tennessee, western North Carolina, Virginia, Arkansas. Distinct four-season cycle with high summer humidity followed by winter freezing (vapor drive direction reverses seasonally).
- **Core Building Physics & Structural Challenges:**
  - **Continuous Inward Vapor Drive & Condensation Traps:** When hot, humid outdoor air (92°F / 85% RH / 75°F dew point) meets air-conditioned interior gypsum wallboard (70°F), moisture drives inward through brick, stucco, and wood siding. If a low-permeability vapor retarder (vinyl wallpaper or polyethylene) is placed on the interior, water condenses inside the wall cavity, rotting framing and breeding toxic mold.
  - **High-Velocity Hurricane Wind Shear & Uplift:** Coastal building envelopes facing Category 3 to 5 cyclonic wind loads (130 to 180 MPH basic wind speeds under ASCE 7). Roof sheathing pull-through, gable end wall failure, unreinforced soffit blowouts, and projectile window penetration.
  - **Vented Crawlspace Moisture Accumulation ("Crawlspace Sweating"):** Outdoor humid air entering foundation crawlspace vents cools down against 65°F earth and air-conditioned subfloors, driving crawlspace relative humidity to 95%–100%, causing wood decay fungi (dry rot) and cupping hardwood flooring.
  - **High Latent-to-Sensible Cooling Ratios:** Modern tight building envelopes reduce sensible cooling loads (heat), but humidity loads (latent) remain high. Oversized air conditioners cool the air too quickly without running long enough to wring out moisture, creating cold, clammy 70°F indoor air at 70% RH.
  - **Subterranean Termite Infestations (Formosan Termites):** Aggressive wood-destroying organisms thriving in warm, damp coastal soils, eating untreated structural lumber from the mudsill upward.
  - **High Groundwater Tables & Hydrostatic Slab Moisture:** Shallow coastal water tables pushing moisture upward through uninsulated, punctured, or missing concrete slab vapor retarders, ruining hardwood, laminate, and LVP adhesives.

## 2. Dominant Regional Architectural Vernacular & Housing Archetypes

1. **Southern Elevated Pier-and-Beam / Shotgun (1880–1940):** New Orleans, Savannah, Charleston, rural South. Raised 2 to 4 feet on brick or concrete piers to escape storm surge and provide open ground ventilation; susceptible to uninsulated floor drafts, termite tunnels, and wood rot.
2. **Florida Concrete Block Stucco (CBS) Slab-on-Grade (1950–Present):** Orlando, Tampa, Miami, Jacksonville. Concrete masonry unit (CMU) exterior walls with vertical rebar tie-columns and poured bond beams, wood-framed gable or hip roof trusses, interior 1x2 furring strips with foil/insulation.
3. **Piedmont & Coastal Plain Brick Veneer Ranch (1960–1990):** Atlanta, Charlotte, Raleigh, Birmingham. Wood frame with single-wythe exterior brick veneer over a 1-inch air cavity, vented dirt crawlspace with sweating fiberglass batts.
4. **Lowcountry Vernacular with Deep Porches & Metal Roofs (1800–Present):** High ceilings, continuous covered wrap-around porches providing solar shading, 5:12 pitch standing-seam 5V crimp metal roofing, high-transom cross-ventilation windows.
5. **Modern Wood-Frame Coastal Spec (2000–Present):** Multi-story engineered wood construction in coastal flood zones (FEMA VE/AE zones) on driven timber or concrete pilings with breakaway ground-floor walls.
